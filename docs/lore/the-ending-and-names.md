# The Ending, and the Names That Point to It

Drafted 2026-10-07 for discussion. Sanchit asked for two things: ideas for the ending, which is
still open, and names that are themselves clues, ones a player can only connect at the end. They
belong in one document, because the strongest name idea is also the key to the good ending.

Read with what-the-warden-keeps.md, the-mark.md, the-moon.md and the-nether.md.


## The names

Each idea below is a name the player sees from early on, which means one thing until the end and
something else after it.


### The Archivist's name is how you close the seal

This is the recommended idea, and the most important one.

The Archivist was the Sealer who designed the seal. She tied each of its locks to a gate, and she
marked each lock with one glyph of her own name. The seal is, quite literally, signed with her
name. And she tied the locks to the gates in a scrambled order, so that nobody who simply lit the
gates one after another would ever learn the right sequence by accident.

The voice signs every tablet with her name, because it needs you to trust her. It cannot stop
using her name without giving itself away. So from your very first tablet on the Moon, the answer
is sitting at the bottom of every page.

But it is not quite her name. The voice writes her glyphs in the order the locks break, which is
the order the mark grows on your hand. Her true signature puts the same glyphs in a different
order. You first see that true signature on the burning world, on netherite plates the Observers
could not rewrite. It looks almost the same as the one on the Moon. Almost.

At the end, in the Deep Dark beside the seal, her last mural shows her signing the seal with her
name. Then it clicks. The signature you have been seeing for the entire game is the way to close
it, if you write it her way and not the voice's.

Her name in translation should sound like a real name and carry a quiet hint for anyone who looks
it up.

- **Tacita** (recommended). It is Latin for "the silent one", and the name of an old Roman goddess
  of silence. Silence is what keeps you alive in the Deep Dark, where her body lies. It also ties
  to the Silence armour trim, which vanilla only ever puts in Ancient Cities.
- **Clausa**, Latin for "closed". More direct, and easier to guess.


### The two factions have names you already know

The voice calls the Openers **the Faithful**, and the Sealers **the Breakers**, because the Sealers
broke every portal. That is true, and twisted.

Their real names only show at the end:

- The Sealers called themselves **the Wardens**. The blind guard in the deep carries their name
  because it is the last of them. That gives this story's title, What the Warden Keeps, a second
  meaning.
- The Openers called their great enchanters **Evokers**: those who call something forth. The
  illagers' evokers are the last of them, still calling up fangs and vexes with the voice's power.

So two vanilla mob names turn out to be the names of the ancient factions. Some players will guess
early, and that is fine. The confirmation comes at the seal, where the Sealers' own sign stands
beside the Warden.


### The voice calls the ancients the First

They were not.

It only lands in the bad ending, when the world starts again and you realise this has happened
before. There were people before the ancients, and there will be people after you. The ancients'
name was the voice's lie from the start. See the endings below.


### The voice calls you the Heir

The Sealers have their own sign for someone with the gift: a skeleton rising. They carved it over
the wither skeletons on the burning world, and it means the Returned.

At the end, the Archivist's last mural shows a marked hand, and next to it, the same rising
skeleton. The voice called you the Heir. The Sealers would have called you the Returned, the same
name they gave the wither skeletons.


### The Observers have no name for themselves

They never sensed themselves, so they never needed one. "The Observers" is what the Sealers called
them. The voice never names its own kind, and when it has to, it uses stolen names.


### The rule behind all of it

Every name the voice gives is upside down:

- The Heir is the key.
- The Vault is the seal.
- The Heir's Marks are the broken locks.
- The destroyer was the defenders.
- The Faithful betrayed everyone.
- The Breakers saved what was left.

A player who works this out can go back and reread every tablet in the game correctly.


## The endings

The final choice happens at the seal in the Ancient City, with every gate lit and all seven glyphs
on your hand. What you can choose depends on what you have found.

Note the timing. The ending is far off: it comes after seven worlds, and v1 builds three. But the
clues for it are planted on the Moon and the burning world, which are being built now. So the
ending's mechanism has to be settled before the Moon's tablets are written, because every one of
them carries the signature.


### Ending A: The Eye Opens

This is the voice's ending, and the only one available to a player who never found the truth.

You take the last sliver of the key from the Warden and put your marked hand to the seal. The eye
on your hand opens all the way. The Observers come through, and this side of the universe begins
to go out: the sky first, then the far worlds, then everything.

Then a lone human spawns on a beach in a fresh world, with nothing, and punches a tree. On the
horizon are ruins. They are yours: your base, your gates, worn down by ages.

There is a deeper version of this that answers the very first question in the game, why you remember
nothing. The lone human on the beach is you. The gift brought you back one more time, into the next
world, with nothing left. The Observers forget too, when they go back to the void, so next time
will be the first time for them again. It is a loop, and nobody remembers it.

That would make the opening of the game, waking alone with no memory, the ending of the last one.


### Ending B: The Seal

This is the Sealers' ending. It is only possible if you have found the Archivist's true signature.

You trace her name on the seal, her way. The eye on your hand closes. The seal shuts from every
side at once, because every gate is lit and tied to it, and the Sealers' plan finally works.

It costs the road. Every gate goes dark, forever. The glyphs fade from your hand one by one. The
rot stops: no new villager will ever turn. The cats come back to you.

Afterwards you still have your rockets. You go back to travelling the long way, by fire, the way
you first came, which was always the honest way. That fits the project's own rule that space is
progression, not fast travel.

A possible post-game: new gates of your own, built from netherite, which the Observers cannot
rewrite. Gates with no voice in them.


### Ending C: The Mirror

A secret ending, for players who found everything. It does not seal the Observers out. It gives
them what they actually want.

Everything in the game has been hinting at their real wound. They never wanted to destroy; they
wanted to stop being seen. Cats can see them. Still water is a mirror, and seeing themselves hurts
them more than anything. They had never sensed themselves, so they do not know what they are.

In this ending you open the seal on purpose, but with every gate on the road turned into a mirror.
When the Observers come through, they see themselves, completely, for the first time. And they
understand. They choose to go back, and close the hole from their own side, alone again by choice
instead of by unmaking us.

The rot lifts everywhere. The hollowed things lie down and rest. The Endermen put down their
stones. The marks fade from the illagers' arms, and they are villagers again. The mark fades from
your hand, and the gift goes with it. The gates stay lit, but now they are only gates.

How the player learns to do this is still open. One idea: a child's drawing, found early and easily
dismissed, of a small figure holding up a mirror to a great eye. At the end, you find out the child
who drew it was Tacita.


### Small differences

Like Outer Wilds, the final scenes can change with what you found and did:

- With a tamed cat at the seal, the cat sits beside you and looks at the eye. In Ending A it hisses.
  In Ending C it purrs.
- If you rebuilt the Moon record from the tiles the Endermen carried, the Archivist's mural appears
  in the final scene.
- If you cured zombie villagers along the way, they are in the final scene too.


## What needs deciding

Settled 2026-10-10: **Tacita's name is the way to close the seal.** The voice signs every tablet
with it, in the wrong order. The Moon's eight tablets carry her signature in words and, since
2026-10-11, as a glyph row in the script font under it; the Nether's seven tablets the same, and
the Forge Record (the netherite plates the Monstrosity leaves) carries the true order in glyphs
only. Text: `tools/archive-lore.py`.

The two orders are glyph indices, numbered in gate order (0 is Earth, 1 the Moon, 2 the burning
world, 3 to 6 the later worlds):

- **The voice's order**, signed on every tablet: 0 1 2 3 4 5 6. It is the order the locks break.
- **Tacita's true order**, on the netherite plates, and the way to close the seal: 4 1 6 3 0 5 2.

The true order is story data owned by logic. If it changes, change it here first.
