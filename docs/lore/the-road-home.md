# The Road Home

Rejected by Sanchit on 2026-10-07. It was too disconnected from Minecraft's own world, and the
arch names did not land. It is kept for reference only. The current draft is
what-the-warden-keeps.md.

This is the whole story of Project Ascension, told twice. The first time, from the beginning, the
way it really happened. The second time, the way a player finds it: one world at a time, in
pieces, believing the wrong thing for most of the way.

It is a first draft. Everything in it can change.


## Part One. What really happened


### The cradle

Long before anyone you have ever heard of, a people lived on the blue world. They were
stonecutters. They cut stone the way other people breathe, and they were very good at it. And
when they had cut everything worth cutting on the blue world, they looked up.

They rose by fire. It was slow, and dangerous, and many of them died doing it. The first place
they reached was the grey world that hangs over the blue one, the one you call the Moon. They
called it the Step, because it was the first one.


### The stone that listens

Deep under the Step's dust, they found a pale stone that listens. Speak to it, and some time
later it speaks back. Your own words, in your own voice. An echo. And two pieces cut from the
same vein will echo each other across any distance at all. Whisper to one, and the other
whispers.

It took them a long time to understand what that meant, and longer to use it. They learned to
cut the stone into rings, and to carve a script into each ring that taught the stone to give back
everything it took. Then one of them stepped into a ring at one end of a hall, and stepped out of
its twin at the other end. The first ring had listened to all of her. Its twin had said her back
out loud.

One step, and you were somewhere else.


### The road

There was one rule they could never break. A ring can only take you where a ring already stands.

So every new world had to be reached the old way first. By fire, the long way round, carrying a
seed of the listening stone. Once the seed was cut, and raised, and taught, that world was on the
road, and from then on it was one step away.

They did it world by world, outward from the Step.

A burning world of ash and black rock, where they dug a dark ore out of the deep and learned to
quench it with gold into a metal that would not burn. They called it the Ember.

A wild world, full of hungry things, where they bred their beasts and shaped their keepers. They
called it the Fold.

A world of iron mountains and rivers of fire, where they built the great forge that cut and
taught every ring on the road. They called it the Anvil.

A world of high walls and narrow passes. They called it the Bulwark.

And last, and farthest, a cold world of frozen seas at the very edge of the light. They called it
the Reach.

The road ran from world to world like a chain. To go from the Step to the Reach, you passed
through every ring in between. It felt like one step. It was five.

For a long time, it was wonderful. A child could wake on one world and sleep on another and think
nothing of it. Families spread across the whole road. People worked on the Anvil and ate supper
on the Step. Every few years they held a festival, and every world brought the best thing it had
made. And on the Step, where the blue world fills half the sky, they cut the Archive: a great hall
under the ground, to keep the record of everything they had learned.


### The names

They had a strange way with names.

A child of the road had no name. Children were called little one, or by a nickname, and nobody
minded. You got your name when you were cut into a crew. And a crew was an arch.

At the bottom of an arch stand the piers, which hold everything up. Then the springs, where the
curve begins. Then the wedges, the many stones that make the curve. Then the haunches, which carry
the weight down. Then the crown, the high part. And at the very top, holding all of it together,
the key. Take out the key and the arch falls.

So in every crew there was a Pier, usually the eldest, who held everyone up. A Spring, usually
the youngest, just starting out. Wedges, who did the work. A Haunch, who carried things. A Crown,
who thought, and kept the record. And a Key, who led. You did not have a name of your own. You
were the stone you were in the arch.

The Keys of every world met together as a council, and the Council of Keys ran the road.


### The toll

Here is the thing nobody knew.

An echo is never quite the voice.

Every time a ring listened to you, and its twin said you back out loud, it said you almost
perfectly. Almost. A little was always left behind in the stone. A word you used to know. The name
of a song. The face of someone you met once. Nothing you would miss, because you could not
remember having it.

Everyone crossed, so everyone lost a little, and nobody noticed. For generations, nobody noticed.
People are always forgetting things.

But the people who crossed the most lost the most. Couriers. Record keepers. The crews of the far
rings, who went home on rest days through five rings there and five rings back. And the farthest
world of all was the Reach.


### The Quiet

The Reach went first.

Its ring did not break. It just stopped answering. The people sent through to find out why did
not come back. They had forgotten the way.

Then the Bulwark went quiet. Then the worlds beyond the Anvil were silent altogether, and the inner
worlds were afraid. It was happening from the outside in, ring by ring, a little closer every
season, as if something were walking the road toward them. They called it the Quiet.

Then the tall ones came.

Tall, and dark, and thin as shadows, stepping out of nowhere. They did not need rings. They had
crossed so many times that they were almost all echo, and an echo can bounce. They stepped a
little way, and then a little way more. They carried stones in their arms, and set them down in
strange places, and picked them up again. And they could not bear to be looked in the eye. When
someone met their gaze, they screamed, and struck.

On the Bulwark, the crews built machines to kill them. The machines were very good at it.


### The Anvil

It was the binders on the Anvil who worked it out.

Crown of the Anvil, the binder who taught the stone, sang a little song into a ring, and had it
carried round and round the road, a hundred times through a hundred rings. When it came back,
notes were missing from the end. The same notes, every time.

At the same time, a message arrived from the Ember. It came from an old miner called Wedge, who
had never stepped through a ring in his life, because rings frightened him. He had kept a tally on
his wall. Every member of his crew. How often each of them crossed. What each of them had
forgotten. The ones with the most marks had forgotten the most. The ones who had forgotten the
most were gone. And old Wedge, who had no marks at all, remembered every one of them.

Nothing was walking the road toward them. Nothing ever had been.

The Quiet was them. The tall ones stepping out of the dark were their own people, coming home with
nothing left, carrying stones because the last thing to go is the work of your hands.


### The argument

The Council of Keys met on the Anvil, and argued for a day they did not have.

Key of the Anvil said put out the rings. All of them, now. Every day the road stays open, the tall
ones walk it, and everyone who crosses home loses more. People will be stranded wherever they
stand tonight. Families will be split across worlds forever. Some worlds cannot keep people alive
on their own. Put them out anyway.

Crown of the Anvil said wait. The stone keeps what it takes. It is all still there. Every word,
every name, held in the rings. I can teach a ring to give it back. But it has to be a ring that
has never taken anything, and the whole road has to be lit, so that it can reach everyone at
once. Give me time.

Key of the Anvil said there was no time.

The Council agreed with Key. The order went out to every world that could still hear it. Put out
the rings. All of them. Do not wait for us.


### The children

There was one more thing the Keys decided that day, and it was the only gentle thing they did.

Every child on the road who had never crossed, or hardly ever, was gathered up. The uncut ones.
The nameless little ones. They were put on ships and sent the long way, by fire, to the blue
world, where it had all begun. Home. A few of the old went with them, to look after them, and were
told to let the children forget the road.

The minders carried one seed of the listening stone. They cut a ring from it, deep under the
ground of the blue world, and built a city around it, and they never opened it. That was the
order. The blue world must never join the road.

But Crown of the Anvil had disobeyed. Before the ships left, she had taught that seed backwards:
to give, instead of take. She hid what she had done inside the script, and told only the minders.
When the sky is full of lit rings again, open it. Not before.

The minders set a keeper over the ring. It was made blind, so that no face could ever move it, and
it was set to listen for the road. When it heard every ring singing again, it would stand aside.

Then the children went up into the light, and they did what they had been told. They forgot. The
script, the road, the names, the Step. They started again, with their bare hands.

You are their children's children's children.


### The dark

On every world, the Keys took out their keystones with their own hands, and the rings went dark.

The keystones were given to keepers. The keepers were made on the Fold, out of stone, and out of
people. The ones who were fading, who knew they would soon forget everything, were offered a
choice. Give us your last memory to keep, and you will keep it forever. Many said yes. They became
stone that walks, and remembers one order. Let no one through who cannot open the way.

Everyone else stayed where they stood. Some lived out their lives on the worlds they were stranded
on, and died there. Most had crossed too often already. They faded anyway, slowly, and walked off
into the dark, carrying stones.

And the tall ones kept stepping. A little way, and a little way more, for ages. Not at random.
Always in one direction. Toward the blue world. Toward home.

The minders' city in the deep of the blue world did not last. The ring had been taught. The rest
of the seed had not: the offcuts, the chips, the dust. And stone that is never taught does what
the listening stone always does. It listens. It takes. It gives nothing back. It grew. It drank
what the dying knew, and spread from every death, until it had swallowed the city whole.

You call it sculk.

And then, a very long time later, one of the children's children built a rocket.


## Part Two. How you find it

That is the truth. Here is how the player meets it, which is backwards, in pieces, and wrong for
most of the way.


### Earth

You start with nothing, on a world that feels emptier than it should.

At night, Endermen wander the hills. Tall, dark, picking up blocks and putting them down somewhere
else. You learn not to look them in the eye. You do not wonder about them. Nobody ever does.

Deep underground, you find the Deep Dark. An ancient city, black and blue, overgrown with a
creeping stone that drinks the experience out of anything that dies near it. At the city's heart
stands a giant empty frame, a doorway to nowhere. Something blind guards it, and it hunts by
sound.

In a chest in that city, you might find a scrap of carved tablet. A few lines, signed Pier of the
Cradle. It says: Do not open it. Not yet. When the sky is full of lit rings again, then, and not
before. Until then, be quiet down here. The stone is listening.

It means nothing to you yet. It is also the best advice anyone will ever give you about the Deep
Dark.

To raise a ring of your own, you need what the Deep Dark gives you. Echo shards. The creeping
stone. A compass. And a tendril cut from one of those blind guards. You are taking a piece of the
keeper of the one ring that must never be opened, to build a ring of your own. You do not know
that yet either.

Then you build the rocket, and you go the long way. By fire.


### The Moon

The Moon is airless and grey, and the blue world hangs in its black sky.

Out on the plain, two pale pylons stand over a stair cut into the ground. One of them has fallen.
Below is the Archive. It is lit only by the builders' own writing, glowing in the floor like a
road.

Carved murals line the walls. Tall, robed, faceless figures cutting stone. Raising a ring.
Stepping through it onto a strange new world. The same ring standing on ash, on ice, among
mountains of fire. A map of the road, every world joined by a line of light. Except one line,
toward the blue world, which was cut into the plaster and never filled in.

On pedestals sit tablets, and the tablets are a story about five people.

The first speaks to you directly. To whoever wakes the gate after us. You came the long way, by
fire and by falling. So did we, once, before the rings. This is the Archive. Read slowly. There is
no one left to hurry you.

The second introduces them. The writer is Crown, who keeps the record, which means she goes to
every world on the road and writes everything down. Key leads the crew. Pier holds everyone up,
and complains about it. Haunch carries everything, and has asked for it to be written down that he
is the handsome one. Spring has just been cut into the arch, and has not stopped smiling. And
there is a strange line about their names. In a crew, nobody has a name of their own. You are the
stone you are in the arch.

The third lists the worlds they walk. Ash plains that burn underfoot. Seas under ice. A forge the
size of a mountain range. And Crown mentions that Spring once crossed four worlds before breakfast
for a bet, and won, and could never remember what the bet was.

It is a joke. It is also the first clue in the game, and almost nobody will notice it.

The fourth is about the keepers. A ring left alone is a door left open. So we made keepers. Stone
that walks, and remembers one order. We gave them our keystones to hold. If you are reading this,
you have met them. We are sorry. They were never meant to outlast us. Pier went with them, the
night they were set, and has not come back to complain.

The fifth is the Quiet. The farthest ring stopped answering. Then the next. Then the next. Always
from the outside in, as if something were walking the road toward us. Key has gone through to find
out what it is. She says she will be back before the dust settles.

Then the carving stops. Past the orrery, the murals give way to bare plaster, with guide lines
scratched in and never cut. The last mural shows four rings in a row. The first two are stained
dark, and the dark is creeping in from the edge.

Down a stair, on a landing, is the sixth tablet. The order came from the Council. Put out the ring.
Do not wait. Key is on the other side. We argued for a day we did not have. Haunch took out the
keystone, because I could not, and carried it to the keepers. He has not come back. Spring will
not speak to me.

At the bottom of the stair is a great hall. In its far wall stands a ring. It is the only builders'
stone in the whole Archive that does not glow. Its middle has been bricked up in a hurry, and the
socket at the top, where the keystone should sit, is empty.

Across the hall from it is a small room, whose doorway was walled up from the inside. The wall has
been broken. The rubble has fallen inward. Something broke in.

Inside are the last two tablets.

The seventh says: Spring is gone. He stopped speaking days ago. He stood in the murals hall looking
at the road for hours, and yesterday he asked me my name. This morning he was not here. I have
walled myself in with the record. The air is going. Key, if you come back, knock twice, the way you
always did, so I know it is you. The air is going.

She has written that last line twice. She does not seem to know it.

The eighth faces the broken wall, and through it, the dead ring. Something is in the hall. It did
not come through the ring. The ring is dark, and walled up. It is tall. It will not look at me. It
is carrying a stone. It has stopped outside my wall.

It knocked twice.

That is the end of it. There is no one in the room. There is no body.

So this is what the Moon leaves you with. A crew of five, and you can account for every one of
them. Almost. Key, gone through the ring. Pier and Haunch, gone to the keepers. Spring, wandered
off. Crown, taken by whatever knocked. And outside, in the ruins, Endermen wander the Moon, picking
up stones. You still do not wonder about them.

You fight the keepers in their citadel, a golem and then a guardian, and when the guardian falls it
gives up the Moon's keystone. You set it into the Moon's ring, and the ring lights. You have just
undone the Council's last order, on one world. And you are building the road exactly the way they
did. The long way first. Then the ring.

You believe something came down the road and killed them. So did they.


### The Ember

The third world is fire and black rock, under an open sky. This is where the builders mined the
dark ore, and quenched it with gold into a metal that would not burn. The mine is still here, and
the forges, and tools lying where they were dropped mid-swing.

There are no bodies. You might not notice that yet. You will.

There is one. In the mine boss's hall, an old skeleton sits in a chair facing the dead ring, as if
waiting for someone to come through it. This is old Wedge of the Ember. His tablets tell you he
came to this world on the seed ship, the long way, sixty years before, and never once stepped
through a ring, because they frightened him. His crew laughed at him for it his whole life.

He tells you about them. Third Wedge, who crossed every single day to see her children on the
Step, and who was the first to forget his name. The young ones who could not find the way back to
the mine. One by one, his crew went strange, and wandered off into the ash, carrying stones.

And old Wedge noticed something nobody else did. He was the only one who had never crossed. And he
was the only one who still remembered everybody.

Behind the planet's keeper is his wall. Every name in his crew, with a mark beside it for every
crossing, and a note of what they forgot. If you read it carefully, you can do the sum yourself.
More marks, more forgotten. His last tablet says: I have sent this to the Anvil. They will know
what it means. I am afraid I know what it means.

There is one more line, near the end, that matters later. The ones who wander off always keep one
habit. The last thing they did every day. Third Wedge still hums the song she sang her children.
One of the young ones still knocks on the rock face before he swings, the way he always did.

If you were paying attention on the Moon, that line should stand the hair up on your neck.

The keeper here is a giant of black metal, still tending a furnace no one needs. When it falls, it
gives up the Ember's keystone.


### The Fold

The fourth world is wild, and full of things with too many teeth. They are the builders' beasts,
bred here long ago and gone feral. The builders shaped their keepers here too, and the shapers'
halls still stand.

The tablets here are written by Spring of the Fold, a young shaper, and they tell you how a keeper
is made. Stone is the easy part. A keeper needs a mind, and you cannot cut a mind out of rock. So
the shapers went to the ones who were fading, the ones who knew they were losing everything, and
asked them a question. Would you like to keep one thing forever? Give us your last memory, and you
will never lose it. Many said yes.

There is a list of them. Two of the names on it, you already know. Pier of the Step. Haunch of the
Step.

You have been killing them. The golem and the guardian on the Moon were Pier and Haunch. And the
keystone Haunch carried to the keepers, the one Crown could not bring herself to take out, is the
key you used to light the Moon's ring. The keepers were never monsters guarding a tomb. They were
the bravest of the builders, keeping the one thing they promised to remember. Let no one through
who cannot open the way. You opened it. That is what they were waiting for.

The deepest tablet on the Fold is about one last keeper. The Council asked us for a keeper for a
ring on the blue world. A ring that must never be opened. We made it blind, so that no face could
move it. We set it to listen for the road. When it hears every ring singing again, it will stand
aside.

That is the Warden. And this tablet is the one that used to be called The Sealing.


### The Anvil

The fifth world is the forge. Iron mountains, rivers of fire, and in the middle of it all the great
forge that cut and taught every ring on the road, cold since the night the rings went out. You
have to bring it back to life. When you do, the Anvil's records open, and this is where the answer
is. It is written as an argument.

First, the song. Crown of the Anvil's little song, sent a hundred times round the road, and coming
back with the same notes missing from the end. Beside it, old Wedge's tally, arrived from the
Ember. Two proofs, from two people who never met.

Then the Council of Keys. Key of the Anvil: put out the rings, all of them, now. Crown of the
Anvil: wait, the stone keeps what it takes, I can teach a ring to give it back, give me time. And
Key again: there is no time. Every day the road is open, more of us become them.

Them. That is the word that turns the whole game over.

Nothing was walking the road toward them. It was them. The Quiet was their own people, coming home
with nothing left. That is why there are no bodies anywhere on the road. Almost nobody died. They
walked away.

The Endermen on the Moon, carrying stones through the ruins, are the builders. The thing that
knocked twice on Crown's wall was Key, who went through the ring to find out what was wrong,
crossed too many times, and came home. She remembered the knock. Nothing else.

And the Endermen on your own world, at night, carrying blocks across the hills. Those too.

Then the order. Put out the rings. All of them. Do not wait for us. This is the tablet that used to
be called The Last Order, and now, finally, you know why they gave it.

And then it gets worse. Every ring you have lit, you have stepped through. Every step has cost you
something. You have no idea what. That is how it works.

But Crown of the Anvil left one more note, hidden in the forge's own script, for whoever lit it
again. She says she disobeyed. She taught a seed backwards and sent it with the children to the
blue world. It will give back everything the road ever took, to everyone it took it from. But only
if it has never been opened. And only if every ring on the road is lit, so it can reach them all.
Key will be angry, she writes. Key is always angry when I am right.

So the thing you were afraid of doing, relighting the whole road, the thing the builders gave
everything to stop, is the only way to undo it. You are not breaking their last order. You are
finishing her work.


### The Bulwark

The sixth world is walls and passes, and the war is still going.

The Bulwark built machines to kill the tall ones coming out of the dark, and the machines are still
here, still firing at anything that steps without a ring. You will see them shooting at Endermen.
This time, you will understand what you are watching.

The general's records are the saddest in the game. Key of the Bulwark led the war. Her son was a
courier, who crossed every day. One day he came out of the dark with the others, tall and thin and
carrying a stone, humming the song she used to sing him.

She ordered the machines to stand down. They did not. They had been built never to obey anyone who
had crossed, in case the order came from one of the tall ones. By then, that meant everyone.

So the machines are still fighting a war against their own people, for no one. And they will
shoot at you too, because you have crossed. You take their guns from them. You will need them.


### The Reach

The seventh world is the farthest, and the first to go quiet. It is cold and dim, frozen seas at
the edge of the light.

When you come down through its sky, you see lines on the ground. Long, straight lines of stones,
thousands of them, set down one after another across the ice. Every line points the same way. Up.
At one small blue light in the sky.

Every Enderman on the road has been walking toward the blue world for ages, a little way at a time,
setting down a stone and picking up another. They are going home.

In the Reach's ring hall, you find the last tablet Key ever wrote. It is addressed to Crown.

Crown. I found them. The Reach crew is here, all of them, and nothing is walking the road toward
us. Nothing ever was. Every ring keeps a little of you, and they crossed so many times. They do not
know me. They are laying stones in lines, all pointing at the blue world. They are going home,
Crown. Not the Step. Home, before the Step. I think I am going with them. I can feel it going. I
have to tell you the most important thing, before I forget it. The most important thing is

And it stops.

Not because something took her. Because she forgot what she was writing.

On the Moon, that sentence would have been a horror story. Here, it is just a woman losing her
words. This is the tablet that used to be called Unfinished.

The Reach's keeper gives up the last keystone. Every ring on the road is lit.

And very far away, under the ground of your own world, something blind is listening.


### Home

You step through your own ring onto your own world, and go down into the Deep Dark one last time.

The Warden is waiting at the heart of the ancient city, in front of the frame that was never
opened. It is blind. It is listening. And for the first time since it was made, it can hear every
ring on the road singing at once.

It turns. And it stands aside.

Behind it, the frame is cut with Crown of the Anvil's script, taught backwards. And past it, where
the keeper always stood, is the minders' last tablet, signed Pier of the Cradle. We have brought
the children home. We were all born here once, a long time ago, before the Step. They will not
remember. That was the point.

You set your last keystone. You open the ring.

It does not take you anywhere. It gives.

Everything the road ever took comes back down it, all at once, from every ring on every world, to
everyone it was taken from. Every word. Every song. Every name. Some of it comes to you. Small
things you did not know you had lost. A face. A tune. The way back from a place you went to only
once.

And everywhere on the road, the tall ones stop walking.

On the Moon, two of them stand in the Archive's gate hall. They put down the stones they have been
carrying. One of them reaches out and knocks twice on the dead ring, very gently, and the other one
laughs.

On the Reach, a tablet that ended in the middle of a sentence finishes it. The most important thing
is that nothing is lost. It is all still in the stone. Come and get us.

And on your own world, all around you, in the dark under the ground and out on the hills under the
stars, the Endermen turn, and look you in the eye, and do not look away.

They remember now. They know where they are. They were home the whole time.

And they know who you are. A child of the road who was never cut into an arch. The one who carried
every keystone on the road, back to where it belonged. The stone at the very top, that holds the
whole arch together.

So they give you your name, at last.

Key.
