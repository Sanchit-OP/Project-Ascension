# The Burning World

Planet 3, the Nether world, in depth. Drafted 2026-10-07. The Moon set the trap. The burning world
holds the next piece: what the Observers promised the ancients, what the Wither is, why piglins
need gold, and the first real horror about the player. Read with what-the-warden-keeps.md,
the-mark.md and the-moon.md.

Everything vanilla that lives in the Nether comes here with the biomes, and every piece of it is
given a reason below.


## What the burning world really was

It was the ancients' first colony, and the place they were richest.

They reached it with the very first portals, the obsidian ones, long before they had the gates.
They came for gold, which runs through the rock here like water, and for something stranger deep
down: a dark, heavy debris that no forge on Earth had ever seen.

The debris is what folding space leaves behind. Wherever the ancients folded the world, a little of
the void scorched into the rock and set into metal. The folding started here, so most of it lies
here. Smelted with gold, it becomes netherite. That makes netherite the void held still by gold,
and the one material the Observers cannot rewrite. Remember that. It matters for the ending.

The colony's people were miners, smiths and, above all, enchanters. The burning world's forges
were where the ancients' best enchanting was done. So when the Observers woke and the voice began
to speak through the script, it spoke here first, to the people who used the script the most.

The colony's leaders listened. They became the Openers.


## The bargain

The voice offered the Openers two things.

The first was a weapon. It taught them to bind their dead soldiers' skulls to the sand their dead
were buried in, and wake something that withers whatever it touches. That is the Wither. Its
recipe is the Observers' design. Every player who builds a Wither is following the voice's
instructions, exactly as the Openers did.

The second was a gift for their champions: to come back from death.

Yes, the same gift. The player is not the first to have it.


## The last war, and what the gift does to you

When the Sealers came to put out the burning world's gate, the colony fought them. It was the last
war, and the Sealers could not win it, because the Openers' champions kept coming back.

But every return cost them something. Each time they came back, they came back a little more burnt,
a little less themselves. The Sealers did not defeat the champions. The gift wore them away, death
by death, until nothing was left of them but black bone and fire, still coming back, still
fighting, with no one inside.

They are the wither skeletons.

The Sealers could not kill them, so they built fortresses around them. The Nether fortresses are
not defences. They are prisons, built to keep the returned in, and the blazes are the Sealers'
living fire, set to burn anything that tries to leave. That is why wither skeletons only ever walk
inside the fortresses.

This is the burning world's horror, and it lands on the player. You come back from death too. Every
time you die and wake in your bed, you have been using the same gift. The burning world shows you
where it ends. The touch of a wither skeleton withers you because withering is what the gift was
doing to them all along.

Whether the gift is costing the player anything yet is a question for the later worlds. Here, the
player only has to realise what they are looking at.


## Why beds explode here

Every Minecraft player has blown themselves up trying to sleep in the Nether.

After the war, the Sealers made sure no one could ever again bind their return to the burning
world. A bed is where you bind it. Try it here, and the burning world burns you.

The Openers had an answer: the respawn anchor, made of crying obsidian and glowstone. Crying
obsidian is portal stone that touched the void, and it has wept ever since. Charging an anchor is
using the Openers' own tool to force the gift to work where the Sealers forbade it.


## The colonists who were left, and why piglins need gold

Most of the colony were not Openers. They were ordinary miners and their families, and when the
gate went dark they were stranded.

Gold had always been the burning world's wealth. In the war it became something more. The colonists
learned that the rot cannot hold gold, so gold became the ward. And they made one rule to survive:
anyone not wearing gold might be rotted. Treat them as rotted.

Over many generations the colonists changed, the way the villagers changed on Earth, and became the
piglins. They kept the rule and forgot the reason. That is why a piglin attacks you unless you
wear gold. It is not greed. To a piglin, someone without gold is someone the rot might already
have.

It explains the rest of their behaviour too:

- Piglins barter: they will give you old things for gold, because gold is life to them, and the old
  things are just things. Among what they trade are crying obsidian and ender pearls, relics of
  the portal days.
- Piglin brutes are the descendants of the guards who enforced the rule.
- Bastions are the colonists' gold-ward strongholds, and the treasure rooms are the ward hoard.
- Zombified piglins are colonists the rot took during the war, still wandering.
- Piglins are afraid of soul fire, because soul fire is what the Sealers burned the returned with,
  and the colonists still remember the war's fires.
- Piglins rot the moment they come to Earth, because Earth is where the leak is.

Hoglins were the colony's livestock. They shy away from warped fungus because the warped forest is
Observer-touched, and they turn into zoglins on Earth for the same reason the piglins rot.


## Every piece of the burning world, and what it means


### The warped forest

Teal fungus, teal light, and Endermen.

The teal is the same teal as the gate script. This is where the colony's gate stood, and where the
rot came through. The Observers' attention warped the forest around it, and it has glowed in their
colour ever since. The colony's porters, the Endermen, were stranded here when the gate went dark,
and they still gather here, as close as they can get to the dead gate.

The burning world's gate ruin should stand in the warped forest. That is a placement decision for
when we build it.


### The crimson forest

Red fungus and the piglins' hunting grounds. It is the colony's farmland, gone wild over the ages.


### The soul sand valley

Blue fire, grey sand, giant bones, skeletons and ghasts.

This is the battlefield of the last war. The sand is full of faces because it is full of the dead.
The giant fossils are the great beasts that fought in it. Soul fire burns blue because it burns
with what the dead remember, and it is the fire the Sealers used on the returned.


### The basalt deltas

Black pillars, ash and magma cubes. The colony's slag fields, where the forges dumped what they
did not want, and where the heat has been brewing small, hungry things ever since.


### Ghasts

Huge, white and crying. They were the colony's great carriers, flying cargo over the lava seas. The
rot took them in the war. They cry because something of the old creature is still inside, and
their tears still heal, the one kindness left in them.


### Striders

They walk on lava and shiver out of it. They were the colony's mounts for crossing the lava seas,
and they will still carry a rider. If the burning world's traversal challenge is crossing lava,
the striders are how the colonists did it, and the lore tells you so.


### Blazes

The Sealers' living fire, set in the fortresses to burn anything that tries to leave. Their rods
make the brewing stand, and the brewing stand makes the potion of weakness, which is half the cure
for the rot. So the cure for zombie villagers needs the burning world. The Sealers' fire is in it.


### Ancient debris and netherite

The void, scorched into metal where the ancients folded space, and held still by gold. Netherite
cannot burn, floats on lava, and cannot be rewritten by the Observers. The Sealers made the keepers'
hearts and the gate keys from it, and wrote their few true words on it.

The netherite upgrade template, found in bastions, is a Sealer record in pictures, preserved by the
piglins without their knowing what it is.


### The Netherite Monstrosity

The burning world's keeper, in its forge.

The voice calls it a monstrosity squatting in the ancients' forge, and that is the name the player
learns. The truth: it is the Sealers' greatest keeper, built of netherite, set over the forge where
the keepers' hearts and the gate keys were made. It still tends the forge fire, because that is
the one order it was given. It holds the burning world's key, and when it falls, the third glyph
appears on your hand.

Behind it, on netherite plates the Observers could not rewrite, the Sealers recorded how the keys
were forged. Among the plates is a piece of the Sealers' cipher, and a name signed in glyphs. It is
the Archivist's name, the same signature that sits at the foot of every tablet on the Moon. See
the-ending-and-names.md for why that matters.


### The Wither

Built by the player, from soul sand and three wither skeleton skulls. The Openers' weapon, the
Observers' design. Building it is following the voice's recipe. Its touch withers, because it is
made of the returned.


## What the voice tells you here

The burning world has tablets too, in the colony ruins, signed as the Archivist like the ones on the
Moon.

The voice tells you this was the ancients' richest colony and their greatest forge. The piglins are
thieves who stole the ancients' gold. The fortresses were the ancients' strongholds, overrun by the
destroyer. A monstrosity squats in the forge and holds the key. The black metal was always the
ancients', and so it is yours. Take it.

Every line pushes you deeper into the colony and toward the key. Every line is upside down.


## The cracks

- The fortresses' walls are built to keep something in. Their arrow slits face inward. That is a
  detail for our own Sealer ruins here, since vanilla fortresses have none.
- A bastion mural shows colonists in gold turning away a figure without it. They are not thieves.
  They are afraid.
- In the soul sand valley, a Sealer carving shows a soldier falling, rising, falling and rising
  again, darker each time, until only a black skeleton stands up. The voice has nothing to say
  about it.
- On the netherite plates behind the keeper, the Archivist's signature looks almost the same as on
  the Moon's tablets. Almost.


## The burning world's piece of the whole story

- **The voice:** where it first spoke, what it promised, and what it taught: the Wither and the gift.
- **Who you are:** the gift existed before you, and the wither skeletons are where it ends.
- **The rot and the ward:** gold, the piglins' rule, and the cure needing blaze powder.
- **Enchanting:** the colony's enchanters were the first to listen, and the script's price.
- **The keepers:** their netherite hearts, and the forge that made them.
- **The Sealers' plan:** a piece of the cipher, on netherite.
- **The names:** the Archivist's true signature, on a surface the Observers could not touch.

It points onward with a forge record of the keepers' bodies being grown, not built, on a green world
full of teeth: Planet 4.


## What this means for Planet 3's open questions

Two of M3.3's open questions get a lore answer here, as proposals:

- **Survival rule: wear gold.** The burning world's air carries the rot. Without gold on you, it
  slowly withers you, as it did the colonists. Wear one piece of gold, as the piglins do, and you
  are safe. That also stops piglins attacking you, so the lore, the survival rule and a vanilla
  mechanic are one and the same thing. Netherite contains gold, so netherite gear later counts as
  the ward with real armour, which makes the planet's own reward the upgrade to its own survival
  rule.
- **Traversal: the lava seas, crossed on striders,** the colonists' own mounts.

The alternative survival rule is poisoned air from the war's smoke, using oxygen tanks. It is
cheaper to build, but it says less.


## What this asks of the build

None of this is decided until Sanchit agrees. If it holds, the burning world needs:

- Its gate ruin placed in the warped forest.
- Colony-ruin tablets in the voice, and Sealer carvings in the soul sand valley and the bastions.
- Our own Sealer prison ruins, with arrow slits facing inward, near the vanilla fortresses.
- The netherite plates behind the Monstrosity, with a piece of the cipher and the signature.
- The gold-ward survival rule, if chosen: a check on worn gold and a slow wither without it.
