# What the Warden Keeps

The main story, built from Sanchit's direction of 2026-10-07 and revised the same day with his
answers. It starts from the things every Minecraft player already wonders about, and answers
them. It is an outline for discussion, not finished text.

Two companion documents go deeper: the-mark.md, the mark on the player's hand, and the-moon.md,
every piece of the Moon and what it means.


## The short version

Long before the villagers, the world belonged to people like you. They found a power, and with
it they opened portals, then gates, and spread to the Moon and the planets. They grew rich on
what they found out there.

But every portal folds the world a little. They folded it so often, and so far, that they wore a
hole through the bottom of the world, into the void beneath the bedrock.

Beyond the void there had never been anything that could notice anything. Then something there
sensed the ancients through the hole. They are the Observers. Until that moment they had never
sensed another side to the universe, and had never even sensed themselves. Being seen, and seeing,
told them for the first time that they existed, and they could not bear it.

They want it to stop. They want this side of the universe gone, so that they can go back to being
alone in the void, unseen and unknowing. And the only way through is the hole, which the ancients
sealed.

That is what ended the ancients. And the story the player is told about it, from the very first
clue, is a lie the Observers are telling.


## The questions every player already has

Here is how the story answers the things a normal player asks on their first night.


### Why are there broken portals all over the world?

They are the ancients' portals, and they were smashed on purpose. When the end came, the last
ancients broke every portal on Earth, so that nothing could come through them. That is why you
cannot simply build one and go to the Nether. Some of the obsidian still weeps.


### Why are there zombies?

Because the Observers cannot make anything, and cannot yet come through themselves. But they can
reach. They reach into a living thing and put out whatever in it can see and know, and what is left
keeps walking, moved by them. A zombie is a person with nobody left inside. The ancients called
this the rot.


### Why can a villager turn into a zombie, and why does a golden apple and a potion of weakness cure them?

Because the hole is not fully closed. The rot still leaks up from the deep, and a zombie's bite
carries it. Gold is the one metal the rot cannot hold. The ancients wore it as a ward. Weakness
loosens the rot's grip, and gold drives it out. The cure is the oldest secret the world has
kept, and it was never forgotten, because witches still brew half of it and every farmer knows
the other half.


### Why do piglins and hoglins rot the moment they come to Earth?

Because Earth is where the hole is. The leak is strongest here. Villagers were born under it, and
over many lifetimes they have grown a little resistant. Anything from another world has no
resistance at all, and turns in seconds.


### Why are there skeletons?

Once the flesh is gone, the rot learned to move the bones. Skeletons are the oldest dead, and they
still carry bows because they were soldiers once. Every world has its own kind: strays in the
cold, the bogged in the swamps, wither skeletons in the burning world, where the last war was
fought.


### What are creepers?

The Observers cannot build. They watched the ancients build for a long time, and learned only how
to unbuild. Creepers are what they grew for that. They are silent, they come for whatever you made,
and they come for you.

Creepers are afraid of cats, and so are phantoms, which are the Observers' other creatures.
Phantoms come for people who do not sleep. Nobody yet knows why the Observers want you awake.


### Why are creepers and phantoms afraid of cats?

Because cats are the only creatures that can see the Observers. Not directly: through the things
they have touched. When a cat looks at a creeper, it sees what is looking out of it.

And the Observers hate and fear anything that can see them. They were used to not existing at all.
Before the ancients, they had never sensed another side, or even themselves. Being seen is the one
thing they cannot stand. So their creatures run from cats.

The same rule explains the Endermen. When you meet an Enderman's eyes, you are seeing the Observer
inside it, and it panics and attacks. Wear a carved pumpkin and it cannot see your eyes, so it
stays calm. And Endermen are hurt by water, because still water is a mirror, and the thing inside
cannot bear to see itself. The full story of the Endermen is in the-moon.md.


### Why are villages small, scattered, and silent?

Because the survivors were told to live that way. The few ancients who lived through the end
gathered the last people and gave them rules. Do not write. Do not enchant. Do not dig deep. Do
not build portals. Never gather in great cities. Live small, and stay out of sight.

The Observers learned to read by watching the ancients, and they speak through
written words. So the survivors gave up words altogether. That is why villagers only make sounds,
and never speak to you. Over many, many generations they changed, and became the villagers you
know. They are the descendants of the survivors. You are not.


### Why do villagers build iron golems?

It is the one craft they were allowed to keep. The ancients made great guardians to keep their
gates, out on the Moon and the other worlds. The villagers make small ones out of iron, and they
make them when they are afraid. When you reach the Moon and meet the Ender Golem, you will
recognise the shape.


### Who are the illagers?

The villagers who would not forget. They kept listening to the voice that comes through words,
and it gave them power for it: their evokers raise fangs from the ground and call up vexes. It
also took them over completely. Every illager carries the Observers' full mark on their crossed
forearms. See the-mark.md.

The illagers want the key to the seal. They believe the villagers still have it, so they raid
village after village, looking for it. But the villagers cannot even understand what they are
being asked. They made themselves forget everything, and they no longer remember that it was
their own ancestors who gave the key to the Warden.

When you kill a raid captain and carry the Bad Omen, the illagers think you have the key, and they
come for whatever village you are in.


### What is the Warden, and what is that giant frame in the Ancient City?

The Ancient City is the last thing the ancients built. It is a fortress around the hole. The giant
frame of reinforced deepslate is the seal they set over it.

The Warden is its guard. They made it blind on purpose, because the Observers
deceive through the eyes, and through words. You cannot lie to something that cannot see and
cannot read. It hears, and nothing else.

And the survivors gave it the key: the means to open the seal again. Not to keep the Observers
out. To keep it from us. The Warden is the ancients' safeguard against their own children, so that
nobody would ever walk the road they walked. Then the survivors forgot they had ever done it.

The key is not an object. It was grown into the Warden itself, and every Warden carries a sliver
of it in its tendrils. That is why building a ring on Earth takes a Warden's tendril. Every ring
you build on Earth spends a piece of the key.


### Why does sculk eat your experience?

Sculk is the seal's living lock. It feeds on what the dying knew, to stay shut, and when anything
disturbs it, it shrieks for the Warden. Every time you kill something near a catalyst and watch
the experience vanish into the floor, you are feeding the lock.


### Why does enchanting cost experience, and what are the symbols floating into the table?

The glyphs that drift from the bookshelves into the enchanting table are the script, the same
script that is cut into every gate. The ancients did not invent it. It is the shape of the void.

Every enchantment reaches down into the void and borrows a little of it, and the price is
experience: what you have seen and learned. Before the ancients, nothing down there noticed. The
ancients enchanted everything, and every enchantment nudged something asleep in the dark. Then
they built portals, the biggest enchantments ever made, and then the gates, bigger still. That is
what woke the Observers. In the wake of their power, they awakened it.

Now the Observers are awake, and the script runs both ways. They can read and rewrite anything
written in it. Every enchantment you make, they notice, and they are generous with you, because
they want you strong enough to reach the end.

Bookshelves make the table stronger because the script feeds on words. Lapis is its ink. The
curses are their kind of gift: one you cannot take off, or one that vanishes when you die. Like
the mark.

Sculk is the Sealers' answer. They turned the void's own price against it: sculk takes experience
to keep the seal shut.

And villagers sell you enchanted books because they cannot read them and want them out of the
village. Do not enchant was one of the rules.


### Why is the Deep Dark so dark?

It is the one place on Earth the Observers cannot see into. The darkness that falls when the Warden is
near is not a curse. It is a blindfold. The ancients built the only room in the world where they
cannot watch you, and then they hid their truth inside it.


### Why are there ruins everywhere, and old pots buried in the sand?

The trail ruins, the buried sherds, the desert temples and the ocean monuments are what is left of
the ancients on Earth. Brush the sand off a sherd and you are reading their history. And almost
none of it is written in words. Remember that.


### And why am I here?

You are human, like the ancients. Nobody else in the world is. Villagers will not speak to you.
Golems watch you. You remember nothing. And when you die, you come back. No one else in the world
does.

Why? That is the last question in the game, and the answer is the worst thing in it.

The Observers did it. Coming back from death is the only power they could give you. Anything more
would have corrupted you the way it corrupted the illagers, and a corrupted thing cannot open the
seal. For the same reason, they cannot reach into your mind and make you do what they want. So
they do the only other thing they can: they show you a false story, and let you walk into it.

And they need you to keep coming back. The keepers on every world, and the Warden at the end, were
made to stop exactly what you are doing. Nobody could beat them once. You can die to them a
hundred times and keep trying. That was the gift. That was the point.


## What really happened, in order

The ancients found enchantment: a script that, written with lapis and paid for with experience,
bends the world. With it they did everything. They learned to fold space, and built the first
portals, to the burning world. Then they built greater gates, and reached the Moon, and then
world after world. They made colonies everywhere, and brought home materials Earth had never seen.
They called the gates the road, and it made them rich beyond anything.

They never asked where the power in the script came from. It came from the void, and every
enchantment, every portal and every gate reached a little deeper into it.

Every fold wore the world a little thinner, and the deepest folding was done here, on Earth. One
day they wore through the bottom of the world into the void. Beyond it were the Observers, who had
never sensed anything, not even themselves. Through the hole they sensed the ancients, and for the
first time they knew that they existed. It was unbearable. They wanted it to stop, and the only way
to make it stop was to unmake everything on this side.

They could not come through the hole themselves. But they could reach through it, and the rot came
up out of the deep, and then it went everywhere the road went. The colonies rotted one by one, and
the gates carried it from world to world faster than anyone could run.

The ancients split in two.

The Openers believed they could bargain with the voice. It spoke to them in their own script, and
it promised them more power than they had ever had, if they would only open the way wider.

The Sealers said no. They broke every portal on Earth. They put out the gates, world by world, and
set keepers on them. They built a fortress over the hole, set the seal in it, and made the Warden
to guard it. Then they gathered the last people, gave them the rules, and told them to forget.

The Openers did not forget. They wrote everything down, everywhere, in words. And the Observers
learned to speak in their voice. Anything written in the script, the Observers can rewrite.


## The trap: what the player is told

The first story the player finds is the Openers' story. It is everywhere, it is easy to find, and
it is written in words.

On Earth, a buried tablet in the trail ruins tells you: we were great. We walked among the stars.
Something rose from the far worlds and destroyed us, and a blind beast in the deep stole our key.
Take it back. Then go to the Moon, where our Archive will tell you how to rebuild everything we
lost.

So you kill a Warden and take its tendril, because the tablet told you to, and you build a ring.
The moment it lights, two things happen. A glyph appears on the back of your hand, glowing in the
same script that is cut into the ring. And when you look up at the Moon through a spyglass, there
is a light on it that was not there before. Something up there answered.

So you build a rocket.

On the Moon, the Archive is warm and helpful. Its tablets are signed by the Archivist, an ancient
who seems to be talking straight to you. She explains everything. The destroyer came from the far
planets, from the outside in. The ancients fled home to Earth. The keepers on every world, the
Ender Golem, the Ender Guardian and the rest, are the destroyer's monsters. Relight every gate,
gather every key, and the ancients' power is yours.

She explains the mark on your hand, too. You are the Heir. Every gate you light adds a glyph,
and when all seven shine, the ancients' Vault will open to you. The same glyphs are carved on the
Archive's pylons, and they light up in step with your hand. It feels like solving the mystery.

And it works. Every gate you light makes you richer and stronger. New materials, new gear, new
worlds. You are living the ancients' golden age all over again. Which is exactly the point.


## The cracks

The Sealers left the truth too, but they hid it where the Observers could not reach it: in the
dark, in dust storms, behind the keepers. And they almost never used words, because the Observers
can rewrite anything written in the script. They told it in pictures: murals, sherds, banners and
maps. The rule a sharp player can work out is that every word may lie, and every picture is true.

So the cracks show up as contradictions.

The Archive's tablets say the destruction came from the far worlds, from the outside in. The
Archive's own murals show the dark spreading from the blue world outward.

The tablets call the keepers monsters. A mural behind the keeper shows the Sealers carving it, and
handing it a keystone.

The tablets say the Warden stole the key. A sherd from the deepest ruin shows the survivors giving
it to the Warden, on their knees.

The tablets call the mark on your hand the Heir's Marks. The Sealers carved the same glyphs on the
pillars as a warning: a count of the seal's broken locks. The symbols never changed. Only the words
explaining them did. See the-mark.md.

The Moon is full of cracks like these. Every piece of it is in the-moon.md.


## The horror

The deeper you go, the worse things get, and slowly you realise it is because of you.

Every gate you relight undoes one of the seal's locks. Zombie sieges come more often. Villagers in
a village you saved start turning. The rot shows up on worlds where it was never seen before. The
Warden rises from the deep more and more.

The mark on your hand grows into the illagers' mark. Cats, who used to love you, start to hiss and
run, because they can see what is looking out of your hand.

Then you go back to the Moon, and the tablets you already read have changed. Same plinths,
different words. The voice is rewriting them, and it is getting less careful.

And finally, in the Ancient City, in the dark beside the seal, you find the real Archivist. She
left the Moon on the last ship to help build the seal, and she never left it. Her remains sit in
front of a mural she carved with her own hands: the Sealers closing the hole. Next to it, in the
one place the Observers could never see, are the only plain words the Sealers ever wrote:

Whatever is speaking to you in my name, it is not me.


## The endings

The player chooses at the seal, in the Ancient City, with every gate lit. These are the first two
endings. A third, and the names that lead to them, are in the-ending-and-names.md, which is newer
than this section.

Open the seal. This is what the voice wants, and it is the only ending a player gets if they never
found the hidden truth. You take the Warden's last sliver of the key, put your marked hand to the
frame, and the eye on your hand opens all the way. The Observers come through, and this side of
the universe begins to go out. Then the screen shows an empty world, and a lone human spawning on
a beach with nothing, punching a tree. It has happened before. The world you started in was the
aftermath of the last person who did what you just did.

Close it for good. This ending only exists if you found enough of the Sealers' pictures to know the
order they meant the glyphs to be read in. Their real plan needed every gate lit at once, because
the seal is tied to every gate, and they could never finish it because the gates were dark. You
can. You trace the glyphs on the seal in the Sealers' order, and the eye closes. The seal shuts
from every side at once. It costs the road: every gate goes dark again, forever, and the glyphs
fade from your hand one by one. Possibly it costs you too, and you stay behind as the new Warden.
But the rot stops. No more zombie villagers. The world gets to be ordinary.

A secret third ending is possible later, for players who find everything.


## How the planets connect

Every planet carries pieces of several threads, so the player can go in any order and still find
everything joining up, the way Outer Wilds works.

The threads are: what the ancients built, the rot, the keepers, the voice, the Sealers' hidden
plan, and who you are.

On the Moon, the trap is set: the Archive, the light, the first gate, the first contradiction
between tablets and murals. Endermen and the keepers are introduced. The Moon was the hub of the
whole road, and its keepers are the seal's most important lock. In full in the-moon.md.

The Nether world is the ancients' first colony and the site of the last war. Piglins are the
colonists' descendants. They attack anyone not wearing gold because their ancestors' rule was that
anyone without the ward might be rotted. Wither skeletons are the Openers' champions, who had the
same gift you have and used it until nothing was left. In full in the-nether.md.

The later worlds each add one piece. One shows how the keepers were made. One shows what the
Openers were promised. One is where the rot first arrived through the gates. The farthest world
holds the Sealers' plan to close the hole for good.


## What already fits, from what we have built

The Lunar Archive works as it stands. Its tablets become the voice, and its murals become the
truth. The murals already show the road to the blue world cut and never filled in.

The Earth gate is already crafted from the Deep Dark with a Warden's tendril. That is now the first
step into the trap.

The Moon's keepers already drop the gate key. That is now the player taking keys the Sealers hid.

The gateway's eye motif becomes the Observers' eye. The Sealers carved it on every gate as a
warning, and the voice tells you it was the ancients' symbol.
