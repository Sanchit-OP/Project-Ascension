## Eight rules, borrowed from Outer Wilds and adapted to Minecraft

1. **Three depths, answers only at the bottom.** Every planet has *surface* lore (on the way
   in, raises questions), *mid* lore (found by following a clue) and *deep* lore (behind that
   planet's boss or hardest place). Only deep lore holds answers. A player who rushes a planet
   leaves with questions, never spoilers.
2. **Every inscription points somewhere.** Back (where did this start?) or forward (where do I
   learn more?). That is what turns scattered tablets into one story.
3. **People, not a civilisation.** Named builders with voices, quarrels, jokes and children. The
   cosmic reveal only lands once the player knows somebody. The current Moon draft is all "we";
   it should become a handful of named people.
4. **Each planet tells one small, complete arc** that is satisfying alone and adds up with the
   others in any order (Outer Wilds' three escape pods).
5. **Important facts appear in at least two places,** so missing one ruin never breaks the story.
6. **The lore teaches the game.** A planet's inscriptions explain its hazard, its boss's weakness,
   or how its gate wakes. A clue is worth reading because it *works*.
7. **Show the ending through bodies.** Remains posed where people died: facing the sky, one step
   from a door, around a dead gate. No text needed.
8. **A ledger, not a quest log.** Track only what the player has actually read, and show worlds
   they have only heard of as *rumours*. Outer Wilds' wordless DLC proved atmosphere alone loses
   players; Minecraft's small text space makes this ledger more important for us, not less.

## The spine: what the player learns, and where

The story in four layers, revealed roughly in this order, but each findable out of order:

| Layer | The player learns | Mostly on |
|---|---|---|
| **1. They were here** | A people who crossed every world through gates. Who some of them were. | Earth (a hint), the Moon |
| **2. Something went quiet** | The farthest gates stopped answering, one by one, from the outside in. | Moon (a hint), Planets 3–4 |
| **3. They chose** | The last order to put out the gates; who argued against it; who disobeyed. | Planets 4–5 (the forge world sent the order) |
| **4. Why it matters now** | Why Earth's gate was never opened, and what the player has been doing by relighting them. | Planets 6–7, and back on Earth |

## Planet by planet (sketch)

- **Earth: the hint.**
  - Vanilla's Ancient Cities already have a giant dead portal frame of reinforced deepslate at
    their centre, guarded by a blind Warden. We claim it: **that is the builders' gate on the
    blue world, the one they never opened.** It costs almost nothing.
  - A single inscription fragment in Ancient City loot is the first rumour of "the road".
  - The Warden is the jailer that forgot what it guards (a League nod).
  - The player's own Earth gate is built from that same Deep Dark, which already pays off.
- **Moon: the setup** (surface and mid; one deep thread behind the keepers).
  - Who the builders were, that they spanned worlds, their daily life, and the first hint of the
    Quiet.
  - The orrery shows every gate: the player sees the *size* of the network before knowing what
    went wrong.
  - **The current eight tablets give away too much for a starting point.** "The Last Order",
    "The Sealing" and the unfinished last words are layer-3 and layer-4 material. Proposal: keep
    "The Road", "The Builders", "Many Worlds", "The Keepers" and a first "Quiet" hint on the
    Moon. Move the rest deeper, to later planets, rewritten as named people.
- **Planet 3, the Nether world: the workers who stayed.**
  - The builders' industry: they mined this world, so netherite and its forges are theirs.
  - One small arc: a crew who refused to leave when the gates went out, the Netherite Monstrosity
    as the furnace-keeper still tending their fire, and posed remains at a dead gate they could
    not relight.
  - Deep: the first proof that the Quiet came *through* the gates.
- **Planet 4, the predator world: the line they tried to hold.** The keepers were made here,
  bred to guard the road. Deep: the keepers were made to stop something coming *in*, not to
  stop people going *out*.
- **Planet 5, the forge world: where the last order came from.** The argument as a conversation
  between named people: who wanted to close the gates and who wanted to keep one open. Deep: the
  order was sent, and one gate was left open on purpose.
- **Planet 6, the war-machine world: the war they lost.** Machines still fighting an enemy that
  left long ago. Deep: what the thing *is*, seen from the side that fought it.
- **Planet 7, the farthest gate: where the Quiet began.** Deep: the endgame truth, which points
  back to Earth.

## The big twist to decide: what the player has been doing

Outer Wilds' best move was making the player's core mechanic the ancients' unfinished project.
Ours is obvious and strong: **the builders' last order was to put out the gates. The player's
entire campaign is relighting them.** Every boss key undoes that order one world at a time. So
the endgame question becomes: *was the road closed to keep something out, and have we just
reopened it?*

Three ways to resolve it (Sanchit's call):

1. **The thing needs the road.** The player has been walking it home. The finale is closing it
   again, properly, from the one gate the builders never opened: Earth's.
2. **The builders were wrong.** The Quiet was never an invader; closing the gates is what killed
   them, cut off and alone. Relighting them is the cure, and the player finishes their journey.
3. **The player is the reason Earth's gate stayed shut.** The builders hid their last children on
   the blue world, gate sealed, so the Quiet could not follow. The player *is* their descendant.
   That is why the player starts on Earth, which `story.md` asks for explicitly.

These can combine: 3 explains the start, and 1 or 2 decides the ending.

## How it reaches the player (mechanics we would build)

- **Plinths and tablets:** built (Archive increment 1). Each entry gets a depth and links.
- **The orrery as the ledger:** it already shows gates lit or dark. It could also show **rumour
  markers** for planets the player has only read about, and a count of inscriptions found per
  world. One block, three jobs: progress board, story map, Outer Wilds' rumour mode.
- **Remains:** a decorative "remains" block (art) placed in poses by structure templates.
- **Lore that teaches:** each planet's design brief includes "which inscription teaches the
  hazard, the boss or the gate".
- **Murals:** wordless, for the very first hook on each planet, backed by text deeper in.

## League nods (a light sprinkle, not a theme)

From the research, a handful that fit our story without borrowing Riot's:
- The keepers asked to be *kept*: a fragment hinting some builders bound themselves into guardians
  or weapons. It fits the combat items' tooltips.
- An hourglass-keeper who stopped time in their tower the hour the gates went out: one named
  builder, and the frozen-dust room on the Moon for `zonias_hourglass`.
- A sun sigil cut over an older moon sigil in the Archive: an erased faction, an early mystery.
- "The blind who were set to listen": the Warden as jailer.
- Item tooltips from the research list, for the combat session if it wants them.

**Flag for the combat session:** five item ids are near-spellings of Riot names (`veigos_blade`,
`zonias_hourglass`, `darken_blade`, `darius_axe`, `heartwood_of_pallas`). Fine for development;
worth renaming before any public release. That is their call.
